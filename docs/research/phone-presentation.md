# Presenting a real iPhone from a managed Mac

Evidence checked 15 September 2026. Primary documentation and current Workbench source only; no device, meeting, account or workplace policy was tested. Vendor capabilities below are documented claims, not measured performance.

**Recommended default: operate and speak to the physical iPhone; use USB for the picture; keep the meeting on the Mac.** This avoids an Apple Account match and wireless receiver setup. The missing part in Workbench is the agent's reply audio: its current USB preview is deliberately silent. A visible phone screen is not evidence that the audience can hear, or speak to, the agent.

## Separate the three paths

1. **Control:** fingers/keyboard operate the app on the phone, or Apple's separate iPhone Mirroring controls it from the Mac.
2. **Voice input:** the target phone app must receive a working phone/headset microphone. Workbench Mac dictation is a different job; it does not send microphone audio into an arbitrary iPhone app.
3. **Reply audio:** the agent's playback must reach the audience through an explicitly selected digital sharing route. Hearing it beside the laptop does not establish that remote attendees receive it.

## What “workplace managed” actually changes

- **Account mismatch is distinct from device management.** Apple's current business guide lists iPhone Mirroring among services available with the same Managed Apple Account on both devices. A work account on the Mac and personal account on the phone does not meet the same-account rule. Do not instruct people to sign a personal account into a work Mac as a workaround. [Managed Account service access](https://support.apple.com/guide/business/service-access-with-managed-apple-accounts-axm171b3ee95/1/web/1).
- **MDM can prohibit particular routes.** `allowiPhoneMirroring=false` blocks the relevant endpoint; `allowAirPlayIncomingRequests=false` disables incoming AirPlay on macOS 12.3+. These are policy decisions, not Workbench connection errors to bypass. [Apple restrictions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/devicemanagement/restrictions?changes=latest_min_4_5_7_9_3&language=objc).
- **USB is account-independent, not policy-independent.** Supervised iPhones can disallow pairing with ordinary hosts. Mac accessory approval and iPhone trust may both appear; the cable must support data. Trust allows broader device access than this one preview feature, so use an approved host. [Managed accessory access](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/guide/deployment/depf8a4cb051/web), [connection checks](https://support.apple.com/en-nz/108643), [meaning of Trust](https://support.apple.com/en-au/109054).
- **Meeting audio can add installation friction.** Microsoft's current guide says Mac computer-audio sharing needs its audio driver and restart; a managed Mac may require IT deployment. The same guide states computer sound includes notification sounds. Do not install or silently alter audio devices during a presentation. Mobile direct sharing removes that Mac driver dependency, but adds the concurrency question above. [Teams audio](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings/share-sound-from-your-computer-in-microsoft-teams-meetings-or-live-events).
- **The agent app owns its audio session.** Apple documents that a default `playAndRecord` session interrupts other nonmixable sessions. This explains why a working movie/audio-share demonstration cannot establish compatibility with a microphone-using agent. Workbench cannot change another vendor's iOS session configuration. [Apple audio-session behavior](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/category-swift.struct/playandrecord?changes=_5).

## Current Workbench source facts

- `Sources/StageKit/DemoCapture.swift:25–26`: video-only intent; `:152–164`: video permission; `:170–195`: disables each `.audio` input port and manually connects only video; no audio output. That is a deliberate safety boundary, not a broken volume slider.
- `DemoCapture.swift:147`, `:200–201`, `:203–209`: connection guidance, competing-preview guidance and session teardown already exist. Do not duplicate recovery controls as a new feature.
- `Sources/StageKit/NativePresentationApps.swift:11–15`, `:27–34`: opens the Apple apps in their own windows. It neither configures QuickTime nor embeds/controls iPhone Mirroring; current explanatory text does not surface the voice-agent limitation.
- `docs/product-spec.md:13–23`: separates real-phone input, Mac dictation and Apple Mirroring. `:82` already distinguishes physical/meeting-receiver evidence from synthetic UI evidence.

## Top three minimal improvements, ranked

1. **Make the existing USB flow voice-demo ready through clear routing and one short rehearsal.** Add concise in-context state: “Use and speak to your iPhone. Phone audio is not shared by Workbench.” An expandable “Let the audience hear replies” guide should offer Zoom's built-in cable/audio path or the tested Teams/QuickTime route, identify the selected meeting audio owner, and distinguish picture connection from receiver-confirmed sound. Label Mirroring as unsuitable when the phone app needs its microphone. This is the strongest immediate recommendation: small, honest, useful without an unproven audio implementation.
2. **Then investigate explicit USB phone-playback monitoring as the only new media capability.** Prototype against the selected muxed device, never the Mac default microphone; require explicit opt-in, actual audio-present feedback, mute/volume, and teardown on End/disconnect. Apple exposes `AVCaptureAudioPreviewOutput`, but the public class alone does not establish which iPhone signal is provided or whether a given agent can still listen. Prove the source first. Do not simply enable every disabled audio port or add a virtual driver/ReplayKit app/network server. [Apple capture output](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/capture-setup).
3. **Make the fallback explicit and bounded.** When USB trust or policy blocks capture, offer the existing Apple-app route and instructions for direct Teams/Zoom phone sharing. Retain a user-selected demo clip for a live-service failure if the presenter already has one; do not automatically record or upload the phone. Share/end ownership must stay clear: ending Workbench cannot claim it stopped a separate meeting broadcast.

## The acceptance test that changes the recommendation

On the actual approved Mac, phone, agent app and meeting client: begin a call; start the chosen picture route; ask the agent a question; interrupt its reply and ask another; have a remote participant confirm legible screen, presenter voice and agent reply with no doubled sound. Repeat after an ordinary phone lock/unlock or USB reconnect. Stop and verify media stops and no microphone route remains captured. Record app/OS versions and route, not private conversation content.

Unknown until that test: the agent's microphone mode, USB muxed-audio signal, Bluetooth/headset routing, muted-versus-disconnected mobile meeting behavior, app playback-capture restrictions, actual managed-device policies, and the meeting client's capture of this rendered surface. Speaker-to-laptop-mic pickup is only an emergency acoustic fallback: noise suppression, echo cancellation and feedback can make it fail. No route here is claimed universally reliable for all AI voice apps.
