# Ambient scenes: a little life in the background

Research and source specification, **15 September 2026**. Three original layered starters are implemented on the `feature/ambient-starter-scenes` branch. The native Mac presentation and iPhone Simulator editor are verified as recorded below; physical and release evidence remains separate. This is not a release announcement. The [visual contract](../../site/handbook/contract.json) owns Mac capability status; the [mobile specification](../ios-preview.md) owns iPhone/iPad scope. The [scene contract](personal-scenes.md) retains ownership of editing, transfer and recovery.

## The useful first increment

Choose a calm picture that works as a still. Let one small part move while the room, device and artwork remain steady. The same saved scene can support a presentation or a personal desktop, with an explicit choice of destination. Local creation, editing and file exchange require no Workbench account or cloud service.

| Starter | Authored movement | Stays still |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Window light (`window-light`) | A translucent cloud detail drifts within the office window region. | Room, window surround, furniture and foreground artwork. |
| Campus breeze (`campus-breeze`) | Two placed copies of an eucalyptus branch detail sway slightly at different periods. | Campus buildings, ground and the rest of the photograph. |
| Coastal sky (`coastal-sky`) | Clouds move within the sky region. | Coastline, **water**, buildings and foreground artwork. |

These are native Core Animation compositions made from original generated image assets. They are not captured nature video, water simulation, depth reconstruction or a 4K media catalogue. Each has a complete still poster, clean plate and transparent detail. The [original asset provenance](../../Resources/AmbientScenes/provenance.json) records the source files and hashes; posters come from the same native composition. No Apple aerial or competitor artwork is bundled.

The existing [gentle photograph motion](../gentle-motion.md) remains an option for a person's ordinary photo: a slow scale change. A layered starter uses its localized effect instead of adding that whole-photo scale. Choosing a motion starter is an explicit opt-in; existing scenes remain unchanged. A still result is useful in its own right.

## Three priorities, in order

1. **Make the chosen scene look good at its actual crop.** Start with three distinct scenes, useful negative space and stable architecture. Preview before use; preserve the original and placed device/logo/persona. Check portrait, landscape and ultrawide crops. A larger feed does not solve an awkward composition.
2. **Make the destination and stopping predictable.** Distinguish an OS-owned still from motion that runs inside Workbench. Pause returns to the authored poster; Stop removes the Mac desktop layer. Respect later manual wallpaper choices, independent presentations, display changes and sleep. Measure those outcomes rather than promise zero battery cost.
3. **Keep motion quiet and optional.** Silent scenes, still galleries and accessible Play/Pause. Stop when lifecycle, accessibility or power policy makes motion ineligible. Keep crop editing still. No daily notifications, smart lights, public feed or general effects editor is needed for this increment.

These priorities are our design conclusions from the sources below, not a ranking established by a controlled competitor experiment. The first is the recommended product investment; the other two are conditions for dependable use.

The public [`NSWorkspace.setDesktopImageURL`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/appkit/nsworkspace/setdesktopimageurl(_:for:options:)) API sets an image for a screen. Neither establishes a public installer for an arbitrary continuous video wallpaper. A native app may render its own desktop-level window without such an installer. [Mac wallpaper guide](https://support.apple.com/en-au/guide/mac-help/mchlp3013/mac), [window levels](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coregraphics/cgwindowlevelkey).

Apple's [iPhone Live Photo instructions](https://support.apple.com/en-au/120734) describe wake playback on iOS 17+. Its [iPhone wallpaper guide](https://support.apple.com/en-au/102638) describes spatial scenes on iPhone 12 or later with eligible photos. The **iPadOS 26** guide also documents eligible Live Photo wake playback and spatial scenes on supported models; it is inaccurate to exclude iPad categorically. Spatial effects are not continuous leaf/cloud animation, and a wallpaper pair does not make Workbench's rig run on the Home Screen. [Pinned iPadOS 26 guide](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/ipad/ipad782d4de8/26/ipados/26).

Apple provides a separate [`ScreenSaverView`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/screensaver) host. Watch face sharing, Apple TV screen savers and visionOS immersive spaces each have other constraints; they are deferred, not evidence for an additional Workbench export promise. Apple's documented Photos-to-wallpaper route remains the shortest supported user handoff. [Apple wallpaper instructions](https://support.apple.com/en-au/102638).

## Native implementation and preservation

The implementation deliberately chooses image layers before video. [`AmbientPhotoLayer`](../../Sources/SceneSyncKit/AmbientPhotoLayer.swift) owns trusted normalized photo-space geometry and small Core Animation transforms. It reuses decoded images; it does not generate new media every frame. Cropping transforms the complete photograph group. The poster and rest pose use the same composition; labels and device video remain outside that group's animation.

[`SceneAmbience`](../../Sources/SceneSyncKit/SceneAmbience.swift) v1 contains only the allowlisted preset, clean-plate hash and detail hash. Imported metadata cannot supply scripts, URLs or animation code. [`PortableScene.assets`](../../Sources/SceneSyncKit/SceneDocument.swift) includes those images alongside all existing artwork and original-recovery assets. Recipe-bearing packages use **version 2** with an exact asset set. Version 1 still packages remain readable; v1 carrying a recipe, unknown versions and unknown presets are rejected with originals preserved.

The first recipe-bearing local commit advances the archive to v2. [`SceneLibraryStore`](../../Sources/SceneSyncKit/SceneLibraryStore.swift) preserves the exact old v1 manifest in a bounded, content-addressed, create-only backup before atomically replacing it. A stale manifest or unsafe backup stops the write. Removing a recipe does not downgrade the archive. Existing bytes, account binding, sync checkpoint and adoption markers remain intact. Old clients fail closed rather than silently discard motion on save; update both peers before exchanging layered scenes. This is not transparent compatibility with older binaries.

The existing limits remain: 12 images, 40 MiB per image, 50 megapixels per image, 90 MB aggregate picture data and 100 MB package. Recovery images count too. A rejected over-budget scene must not lose an original. `.workbenchscene` carries everything without an account; optional personal sync keeps the existing private scene zone and complete-package transport, with no new backend or automatic account choice.

[`MacSceneSync`](../../Sources/StageKit/MacSceneSync.swift) retains canonical recipe hashes while materializing immutable native image caches. A native clear remains a clear. Replacing a backdrop clears/replaces its rig; crop and layout edits retain it; cancellation leaves the original alone. Existing mobile recovery attachments remain independent. These are preservation requirements even when a renderer cannot play the effect.

Unknown recipe/version means **do not open-and-resave that document**; it is not permission to strip the recipe. A known scene whose playback is temporarily unavailable should show its stored poster without losing metadata. Those are distinct failure paths.

The [`GentlePhotoMotion`](../../Sources/SceneSyncKit/GentlePhotoMotion.swift) policy and native hosts retain visibility, Reduce Motion, low-power, thermal and sleep checks. Mac focus alone does not establish invisibility while presenting. The independent desktop controller checks its applied-still ownership and stops on conflicting changes. No autoplay sound, keep-awake request, login agent or background delivery guarantee is added. [Apple Reduce Motion](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/accessibilityreducemotion), [scene phase](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/scenephase/active).

Apple's separate [animated-image autoplay preference](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/accessibilityplayanimatedimages) is observed by the current Mac and iOS hosts. The mobile policy test verifies suppression; actual toggling of the OS setting remains a separate hardware check. Energy and comfort remain measurements to perform, not benefits established by choosing Core Animation.

A short local video could later improve water or complex canopy realism using [AVFoundation playback](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avplayerlooper). App-owned playback does **not** require a public OS video-wallpaper installer. It would require its own bounded media validation, poster agreement, codec/track rules and lifecycle/energy proof. Video export and generated Live Photos are deferred. An external hypothetical design critique usefully challenged naming, seams and poster parity; it did not verify product behavior or App Store policy, and its OS-installer prerequisite for native video was rejected.

Distributed media must be original or licensed for redistribution. Buying wallpaper does not imply that right: [24 Hour Wallpaper's terms](https://24hourwallpaper.com/sla/) restrict other use. This research found no general redistribution grant for Apple's aerials; the [macOS licence](https://www.apple.com/legal/sla/docs/macOSTahoe.pdf) is not such a grant. Workbench's generated originals and source provenance avoid that dependency.
