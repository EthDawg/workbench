# Present's phone link

Reviewed 6 October 2026 against the Present rebuild source ([issue #276](https://github.com/Ship-Work/workbench/issues/276)). This record owns how Present finds, shows and explains your phone. The [structured visual contract](../site/handbook/contract.json) owns capability status and acceptance (`phone-link`, `scene-session`, `compact-present-preparation`). The [rebuild ledger](research/2026-10-06-present-rebuild.md) records what the rebuild removed, why, and what would bring it back. The [guide](https://workbench-mac.vercel.app/guide/#phone-share) has the short version for people using the app.

## Outcome

Your phone on a clean stage, in your call. There are two ways in. The first is USB into Workbench's stage: plug an iPhone or iPad into the Mac with a data cable, open Present, and the phone appears inside the saved scene on the Present page before anything is pressed; Present opens the stage already live, you share that Workbench window in Teams or Zoom, and you use the phone itself. The second is for a Mac that cannot receive the phone, such as a managed work Mac that blocks phones as accessories: share the phone's screen from the Teams or Zoom app on the phone, without the stage. Apple's iPhone Mirroring is offered only where it is installed, and only works with the same Apple Account on both devices, with the phone's microphone and camera off. QuickTime Player is a check, not a route: if it cannot see the phone either, the Mac is not receiving it, and the cause is the cable, Trust or a policy rather than Workbench. Workbench stays a video-only compositor. It adds no phone audio, Mac input to the phone, driver, wireless receiver, meeting integration or policy detection.

## The status table

`PhoneLink.status` turns the signals into one status: a title, an optional detail and at most one next step. Every Present surface shows these words and nothing else about the phone. The signals are the Apple phones and tablets on the USB bus, the screen sources macOS offers for capture, the remembered device, the camera permission, the capture session's phase and whether Workbench let the phone go for an Apple app. The session's own phase comes first, then that release, then the permission, then what is available and what was remembered. A `capturing` flag says whether a session is allowed to run at all (the Present page shows a device scene, or a presentation with a device frame runs); without it, an offered screen reads as ready rather than connecting, which is what the headless receipt shows.

In the words below, {noun} is iPhone, iPad or iPod touch when the screen's name or the USB bus says so, phone for another screen and device for a plain video source. {Noun} is the same word at the start of a title (Phone, Device; iPhone and iPad keep their case). No serial number or device identifier appears in any status.

| Phase | When | Title | Detail | Next step | Also offered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `live` | The session is receiving frames | Showing {noun} | Use the phone itself for taps, typing and its own voice features. | None | None |
| `connecting` | The session is opening a device; or, while a session is allowed, the remembered device or the one phone screen is offered and about to be adopted | Connecting to {noun}… | None | None | None |
| `available` | The remembered device or the one phone screen is offered while no session is allowed, as in the headless receipt | {noun} ready | Present shows {source name}. | None | None |
| `stalled` | No frame for five seconds | {Noun} stopped sending its picture | Unlock it or reseat the cable, then Reconnect. | Reconnect | Can’t see your phone? |
| `interrupted` | The shown device went away, or its session hit a runtime error | {Noun} disconnected | Reconnect the cable and unlock it. The stage waits here. | Reconnect | Can’t see your phone? |
| `busy` | macOS said another app holds the device | Another app is using the {noun}’s screen | Close QuickTime Player or the other preview, then Reconnect. | Reconnect | Can’t see your phone? |
| `couldNotOpen` | The device could not be opened as an input, for any other reason | Workbench can’t open the {noun} | Unlock it and close any other preview using it, then Reconnect. | Reconnect | Can’t see your phone? |
| `couldNotStart` | The session configured but never ran; nothing proves another app or Trust | The {noun}’s screen didn’t start | Unlock the phone, close any other app previewing it, then Reconnect. | Reconnect | Can’t see your phone? |
| `accessPending` | The macOS camera prompt is waiting, on first use | Allow device video in the macOS prompt | Workbench shows the phone’s picture only. It never opens the phone’s microphone. | None | None |
| `released` | Workbench let the phone go so QuickTime Player or iPhone Mirroring could use it, and no session runs | Let go for another app | Workbench released the {noun} so QuickTime Player or iPhone Mirroring could use it. Reconnect to show it here again. | Reconnect | None |
| `accessRestricted` | Camera access is restricted on this Mac | Device video is restricted on this Mac | Use a route your organisation allows, or ask IT. | None | Can’t see your phone? |
| `accessDenied` | Camera access was turned off for Workbench | Workbench can’t use device video | Allow Workbench under System Settings › Privacy & Security › Camera, then come back to Present. | Open Camera settings | Reconnect; Can’t see your phone? |
| `phoneOnUSB` | An iPhone or iPad is on the USB bus, but macOS offers no screen | iPhone connected, screen not available yet (or iPad, iPod touch) | Unlock it and tap Trust on the phone. If this Mac asked to allow the accessory, allow it. | None | Reconnect; Can’t see your phone? |
| `noPhone` | Nothing on the bus and no screen offered | No phone on USB | Connect with a data cable, unlock the phone and trust this Mac. If this Mac asks to allow the accessory, allow it. | None | Can’t see your phone? |
| `waitingForRemembered` | The remembered device is away and other sources are offered | Waiting for your remembered {noun} | Reconnect it, or show {the other source} instead. With several others: Reconnect it, or choose another screen. | Choose screen… | Reconnect; Can’t see your phone? |
| `screenFound` | One plain video device, such as a capture card, and nothing remembered | {source name} found | Show it, and Workbench remembers it. Only phone and tablet screens appear by themselves. | Show {source name} | Reconnect |
| `chooseScreen` | Two or more sources and nothing remembered | {count} screens available | Choose which one to show. | Choose screen… | Reconnect; Can’t see your phone? |

A remembered device that is away while nothing else is offered reads as `phoneOnUSB` or `noPhone`, because the bus is the better clue. A session that is already live is the truth whatever the cached permission says. The symbol follows the phase: a phone when live, a phone with waves while connecting or waiting for the prompt, a crossed camera for restricted or denied access, a crossed connector for no phone, and a connector otherwise.

Each surface draws the same status at its own size. Inside the device frame, the Present page shows the title and step, and the stage shows the title, detail, step and Can’t see your phone? until the phone is live. The row under the page's preview shows the title, detail, step, Reconnect and Can’t see your phone?. The stage's expanded controls show the title, detail, step and Reconnect. The Present menu shows the title as a line, then the step, Reconnect and Can’t See Your Phone?…. Reconnect and the help link appear only where the table offers them. Home's live row reads Presenting · {title} while a presentation shows a phone.

## The journey

**Before.** Open Present with a scene whose Device frame is on. While the page's preview can be seen, the one capture runs: it lists the sources macOS offers, then shows the remembered device, or adopts the one phone or tablet screen and remembers it. On first use macOS asks for camera access, and the status says so. The phone then appears inside the scene's device frame, in its own proportions, before anything is shared. A scene without a device frame starts no capture and says how to add one. Leaving the page, hiding or covering the window, or choosing a scene without a frame stops the session; the remembered device stays. A running presentation keeps the session for its own device frame, and the page's device scene keeps it for the preview, whichever of the two is showing a phone.

**Can’t see your phone?** opens one sheet. It shows the live status; three checks away from Workbench (a cable that carries data, an unlocked phone that trusts this Mac, and the Mac's approval of the accessory, which a managed Mac may block); Check in QuickTime Player, which tells the Mac apart from Workbench; the ways to show the phone in a call without the stage (Zoom on this Mac with Share Screen › iPhone/iPad via Cable and Share computer sound, or sharing from the Teams or Zoom app on the phone with one microphone and speaker live); iPhone Mirroring with its same-Apple-Account condition, with Open only where it is installed; Copy connection details; and Help online. Done and Escape close it. Choose screen… opens Which screen?, which lists every source with the remembered one checked, shows and remembers a choice, and can Look again.

**During.** Present opens the stage in a window by default, or full screen when Options › Start full screen is on; that choice is saved with the scenes. The stage draws the same session through its own layer, so the phone is already live and nothing reconnects. Share the Workbench presentation window in the call and use the phone itself. The edge tile opens controls with the status, the step, Reconnect where it can help, Choose screen… when there is more than one screen, Position and End. Command-R reconnects; Escape closes the controls, then ends. Reconnect and Show from the stage, Command-R, the menu and the live settings all go through the capture's owner, so a phone released for an Apple app comes back from any of them. The Present menu in the menu-bar panel holds the status line, the step, Choose screen (the screens, when there is more than one), Reconnect and Can’t See Your Phone?… where offered, Match Device Proportions, Show Presentation Window, Full Screen, Window Size, Window Position and End Presentation; the floating toolbar's View holds the same actions without the status line and End Presentation. On the page, the button reads Show presentation, and the live presentation block repeats the window controls with the status row. Persona's camera refuses the device Present holds, whether the page or the stage holds it.

**Handing the phone to an Apple app.** From the page's sheet, Check in QuickTime Player or Open closes the sheet, releases the capture on its own queue, and opens the app only after that release. A launch failure shows a notice on the page and starts nothing. While a presentation runs, the sheet reached from the page or from the stage reads End presentation & check in QuickTime Player and End & open, the presentation ends first, and `PresentationHandoff` waits for both the capture release and the stage window's closure, in either order, before one open. A failure there shows an alert and never takes the phone back. Either way the capture stays released, and the status reads Let go for another app with Reconnect, until the person presses Present, chooses Reconnect or shows a screen. Covering the Workbench window, or leaving the page and coming back, does not take the phone back, so the Apple app keeps it while it is in front.

**After.** End, from the controls, the menu, Escape or the window's close button, closes the stage and its controls and releases the keep-awake activity. The capture keeps running only when the Present page is on screen with a device scene, so the phone stays in its preview; otherwise it stops. End does not end a meeting, stop a broadcast started on the phone, close an Apple app, restore a desktop picture or change saved scenes. Quit stops everything Workbench started.

## Rules kept

- **The one phone screen is adopted; nothing else is.** With nothing remembered, a single source that is a phone or tablet screen is shown and remembered without a click. A plain video device, such as a capture card or a camera, and any two sources wait for a choice. This replaces the 15 September rule that every first choice was explicit, and it stays safe for four reasons. It happens on Workbench's own Present page, a preparation view that runs the capture only while it is on screen; nothing reaches a call until the person shares the stage window. First use passes the macOS camera prompt. Only Apple phone and tablet screens qualify, Continuity Camera is excluded, and the screen flag is a display hint, never proof of whose phone it is. Reconnects reopen only the exact remembered device: if it goes away the stage waits, and it never opens another camera or another phone, even another iPhone.
- **Video only.** Audio ports are disabled and the input is wired without automatic connections, so the phone's microphone never opens. There is no recording output, upload or Mac input to the phone. Taps, typing and the phone's own voice features happen on the phone.
- **No policy detection or setting change.** Workbench reads no device-management policy. Restricted camera access names IT; denied access offers Open Camera settings. Never install drivers, sign out of accounts or weaken security to make a demo look ready.
- **One owner per state.** The capture owns the session and the remembered device; `PhoneLinkMonitor` owns the words; scenes, Persona and desktop recovery keep their own owners. Changing a saved scene does not change a running presentation.

## Diagnosis

Two reports carry the same facts, with no serial number or device identifier:

- **Copy connection details** in the help sheet copies the current facts; the button reads Copied for four seconds.
- `--phone-link NEW_FOLDER [SECONDS]` on the app's executable runs the same monitor headless for 1 to 600 seconds (8 by default) and prints each change of status. It reads this edition's remembered device, so the receipt says whether that device is present or absent. Use a folder outside Desktop, Documents and Downloads, such as `~/phone-link`, so macOS asks for no folder access: `open -n -a "/Applications/Workbench.app" --args --phone-link "$HOME/phone-link"`. It refuses a folder that already exists, then writes `phone-link.txt` (the facts, followed by every change seen while watching) and `receipt.json` (the build, the final status, the counts of USB devices and sources, the permission and the changes). It starts no capture session and asks for no permission, so it is safe to run on a managed Mac where the phone never appeared. Run through the signed app with `open -n` and `--args`, it has no terminal output, so the folder is the receipt.

The facts are seven lines: the Workbench build and macOS version; the phones and tablets on the USB bus with their product IDs; the screen sources, each marked screen or video; whether a remembered device is present, absent or none; device video access (authorized, not asked yet, denied or restricted by policy); the session (no session, released for another app, waiting for the permission prompt, connecting, live with its size, stalled, interrupted, busy or could not open); and the status with its detail. Source names are the names macOS shows, which can include the phone's own name, so read a report before sending it outside your organisation.

Reading one: no phone on the bus points to the cable, the port or a blocked accessory; a phone on the bus with no screen source points to unlock, Trust or the Mac's accessory approval; a screen source with a failed session points to another app holding the screen; restricted access is a policy for IT.

## Architecture

`PhoneLink.swift` holds the facts and the words. `PhoneLinkSignals` collects the bus, the screen sources, the remembered device ID, the permission, the capture's `CapturePhase` and whether the phone was `released` for an Apple app. `PhoneLink.status` is the pure reducer behind every title, detail and step above, and `PhoneLink.diagnostic` writes the report. `USBPhoneWatch` reads the bus through IOKit with no permission or pairing: Apple's vendor ID with the phone, tablet and iPod product IDs, then the product name, so keyboards and hubs are dropped. `PhoneLinkMonitor` is the main-actor owner. It mirrors the capture's sources, choice and phase, watches the bus and the permission (refreshed on wake and when the app becomes active) only while Present is in use, starts no capture and asks for no permission. Its `fixture` pins signals for offscreen renders, and `observe(seconds:build:onChange:)` runs the headless receipt.

`DemoCapture.swift` is one video-only AVFoundation session on its own serial queue. It publishes the sources, the remembered `selectedID`, the `phase`, `live`, the video `dimensions` and `heldDeviceID`. Each surface draws the session through its own `AVCaptureVideoPreviewLayer` from `makePreviewLayer()`, so the page and the stage show the same session without a second open. `CaptureRecovery` owns the adoption rule and the generation token that drops frames from an older session. Discovery lists external devices, excludes Continuity Camera and marks muxed sources as screens. A health check every two seconds marks a stall after five seconds without frames; retries back off from two to thirty seconds; wake reconnects. The remembered device lives in `demo-source.json` beside the scenes and survives `stop`, whose completion runs on the main thread only after the queue has released the session and its observers.

`DemoScenes.swift` owns the one `capture` and its `phoneLink`. `setPageVisible`, reported by the page's preview from its window's visibility, the selected scene's Device frame and a running presentation decide `captureWanted`, and `reconsiderCapture` starts or stops the session to match and activates the monitor. `releasedForHandoff` keeps the capture off after an Apple app opens, and tells the monitor so the status reads Let go for another app, until Present is pressed or the person reconnects or shows a screen; visibility never clears it. A running presentation's help goes through the presentation's own handoff. `openNativeApp` and `performPhoneStep` carry out the help sheet's and the status's actions from the page. `startsFullScreen` is saved in `present-preferences.json`, and `startDemo(mode:)` uses it when no mode is given. `heldDeviceID` lets Persona's camera refuse the device Present holds. `restoreDesktop` stays for recovery journals written by earlier versions.

`DemoScenesView.swift` is the page: scenes on the left; the scene heading with Persona… and the scene's actions menu; the preview (`SceneCanvas`, a `DemoStageSurfaceView` with the live layer, drag handles and the visibility report); the status inside the frame while the phone is not live; `PhoneLinkStatusRow` under the preview; View image, the drag hint and Change backdrop…; the Scene options disclosure, which holds the device frame, size and position, logo, backdrop zoom, device shape and hand; and a fixed footer with one Present button, Options (Start full screen, Export image…) and the sharing hint. It presents the help sheet and the Which screen? sheet, and opens an Apple app only after the help sheet has gone.

`DemoPresentation.swift` is the stage window and its lifecycle. Its Reconnect and Show go through closures `DemoScenes` sets, so they reach a capture the owner released. `DemoStageContent` draws the scene through `DemoStageSurfaceView` with a still backdrop and the live layer inside the device frame, the frame's status until the phone is live, the edge tile and its expanded controls, the stage's Which screen? sheet with Match device proportions (on by default) and the video size, and the help sheet with `endsPresentation`. `makeControlsMenu()` builds the Present menu for the panel, and `makeViewMenu()` the toolbar's View. `PhoneLinkStatusRow` is the shared status row. `releaseCapture` hands the shared capture back to `DemoScenes` at End, released for a handoff, and `PresentationHandoff` in `PresentationLifecycle.swift` joins that release with the window's closure before one app open. `offscreenStage` hosts the same content for renders.

`PhoneConnectionHelp.swift` replaces `NativePresentationApps.swift`. `NativePresentationApp` resolves and opens QuickTime Player or iPhone Mirroring through `NSWorkspace` by bundle identifier; it never embeds, automates or captures them, and a missing app is a visible message. `PhoneConnectionSupport` holds the three checks and the guide and Camera settings links. `PhoneConnectionHelp` is the one sheet described above.

`MovingSceneView.swift` remains the still renderer for the page and the stage, with device video between the stationary frame and the foreground branding; motion is never requested on the Mac. `StageKitController` exposes `phoneLink`, `phoneLinkStatus`, `presentationShowsPhone` and `performPhoneStep` so a host surface, such as Home's live row, shows the same words, and `setPhoneLinkFixture` and `addStarterScene` serve offscreen renders.

Removed with the rebuild: `NativePresentationApps.swift` and its six-route guide, `DesktopMotion.swift` with `DesktopMotionTests.swift`, the Gentle motion toggle and Pause/Play controls, and from Present the Use as desktop, Use as animated desktop, Connection & audio… and Switch to Browser Tab… doors. No saved file changes format: scenes keep their `gentleMotion` and `ambience` fields, and `present-preferences.json` is the one new file.

## Verification

Evidence states are kept apart. **Source checked:** the rebuild, its tests and its renders, 6 October 2026, recorded in the [present foundation verification record](verification/2026-10-06-present-foundation/README.md). **Installed:** not yet; no signed Preview carries it. **Hardware:** no phone was on this Mac's USB bus that day, so no phone has been shown by this build.

StageKit tests use synthetic signals only: no device, permission prompt, capture session start or IOKit notification, apart from one read-only listing of the USB bus. They run in `bash scripts/test-stage.sh`, which `bash scripts/test.sh` includes.

- `Tests/StageKitLegacy/PhoneLinkTests.swift`: `testNothingAttachedNamesTheCableAndTheAccessoryPrompt`, `testPhoneOnTheBusWithoutAScreenAsksForUnlockAndTrust`, `testOnePhoneScreenIsAdoptedAndAPlainVideoDeviceWaitsForAClick`, `testSeveralScreensAskForAChoiceAndARememberedAbsentPhoneWaits`, `testRememberedPhonePresentReadsAsConnectingUntilTheSessionSpeaks`, `testSessionPhasesOutrankAvailability`, `testPermissionOutranksAvailabilityAndRestrictedOffersNoToggle`, `testReleasedForAnAppleAppSaysSoUntilReconnect`, `testNounsFollowTheDeviceNotTheSerial`, `testUSBClassificationKeepsPhonesAndDropsOtherAppleDevices`, `testDiagnosticNamesFactsWithoutIdentifiers` and `testMonitorFixtureAndMirrorAreIndependent`. Together they cover every row of the status table, Reconnect and help on representative rows, nouns without serials, USB classification and the report's seven lines.
- `Tests/StageKitLegacy/PhonePresentationTests.swift`: `testOnePhoneScreenIsAdoptedAndALostDeviceNeverSwitches`, `testNativeHandoffWaitsForBothCaptureAndWindowInEitherOrder`, `testHandoffKeepsFirstRequestAndDoesNotRetryFailedLaunchOrOrdinaryClose`, `testHandoffWaitsThroughFailedNativeTransitionAndRepeatedEnd` and `testCaptureStopCompletionRetainsHandoffAfterPresenterRelease`, with a stopped capture and no window.
- `bash scripts/test-stage.sh --phone-guide-fixture` opens the help sheet in four synthetic states, with and without a simulated presentation, behind a fake app-open boundary. It starts no capture and launches no app.

Renders use synthetic scenes and pinned signals, with no capture session: an offscreen preview never reports itself visible. The verification record holds the Present page with no phone, a phone on USB, two screens, video restricted and a narrow window, and the stage with no phone and with two screens, from the StageKit layout pass with `PhoneLinkMonitor.fixture` pinned per state, together with the StageKit suite result for that revision. The surface gallery adds Present with a scene in three pinned states (nothing on USB, an iPhone on USB, video restricted) through `setPhoneLinkFixture` and `addStarterScene`. No render shows a live phone, because none was attached.

Still owed on hardware:

- A real iPhone on this Mac: plug in, unlock and Trust, and watch the page move from No phone on USB to iPhone connected, Connecting and Showing iPhone. Lock it to see the stall, unplug and replug it, and check the first-use camera prompt and that no microphone prompt appears.
- The same on the managed work Mac, including the Mac's accessory prompt, with a `--phone-link` receipt if the phone does not appear.
- Two preview layers on one session: each layer's connection is checked before it is added, and a layer that cannot be wired stays empty instead of failing the session. Whether macOS accepts the stage's second connection for a phone screen while the page holds the first is a hardware check.
- A second display, with the page on one and the stage full screen on the other.
- Teams and Zoom receiver views of the shared stage window, and of a phone shared from its own Teams or Zoom app.
- The help sheet's QuickTime check with a real phone: the capture is released before QuickTime opens, and Present picks the phone up again on return.
- Present opening full screen from the saved Start full screen choice.

Phone audio into a call stays a separate job with its own gate, `phone-voice-duplex`, in the contract.
