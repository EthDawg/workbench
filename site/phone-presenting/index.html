A TWO-WAY CONVERSATION

Hearing it yourself is half the check.

The phone microphone receives your question. The agent produces its reply. Your meeting microphone carries your narration. Shared audio carries the agent’s reply to attendees. These are separate paths; a sound toggle cannot supply audio absent from the chosen route.

  1. 01 · LISTEN

    Speak to the physical phone.

    Start the actual agent. Confirm it hears a question and its reply plays through the intended output. QuickTime’s audio selector does not send a Mac microphone into the phone app.

  2. 02 · CONFIRM

    Ask someone on the other end.

    In the intended Teams or Zoom client, check they see the phone and hear both you and the reply, without echo. Window, whole-display and browser-tab sharing can capture different things.

  3. 03 · CONTINUE

    Interrupt, then reconnect.

    Interrupt the reply and ask a follow-up. Reconnect once and repeat. Ordinary music playback does not establish that this conversation will keep working.

Teams desktop on Mac may need its audio component and a restart; a managed Mac may need IT’s help with that installation. Browser tab audio only covers the shared tab. Use the meeting client’s actual sharing choices and check the receiver. Microsoft’s sound-sharing guidance.

WORK WITH THE MAC YOU HAVE

Prefer an allowed route.

USB avoids matching Apple Accounts and enabling a wireless receiver. It still needs an allowed data connection, trust and video access. Workbench distinguishes access turned off, where Camera settings may help, from access restricted, where an approved alternative or IT help is appropriate.

A managed Mac does not automatically rule out Mirroring. Apple lists it as available with the same Managed Apple Account on both devices; hardware, region and organisation policy still matter. Workbench does not change accounts, permissions, drivers or workplace policy. Managed Apple Account services · Accessory management.

WHEN THE PLAN CHANGES

Leave one capture before opening another.

End preview & open QuickTime Player or iPhone Mirroring dismisses the guide, closes this Workbench presentation and waits for its USB session to release. Then the Apple app opens. A failed launch stays visible and never silently restarts capture.

If the selected phone disappears, Workbench waits for that exact device. It does not choose another attached camera. Source offers another explicit choice; Reconnect retries the current one.

End presentation stops Workbench’s window, capture and keep-awake activity. It keeps your scene and desktop appearance. Stop a separately started phone broadcast or meeting share in the app that owns it.

EXPLORE, THEN CUT BACK

Clarity earns its place.

The priorities were route clarity with safe handoff, a proven phone-audio path, then a wireless receiver. We implemented the first. Apple, Zoom and Teams already supply useful routes for the others; adding an audio engine or receiver before proving a missing user journey would add maintenance without dependable value.

Primary documentation reviewed 15 September 2026. Reflector, AirServer and Camo were also compared for their actual category and constraints. No competitor app was installed or exercised in this review. Claude challenged public source summaries; source checks and native tests determined the accepted implementation. Full comparison.
