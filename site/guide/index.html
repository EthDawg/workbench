CORE UTILITY

Speak into the Mac field you chose.

  1. Choose a destination. Click a field in a Mac app or browser, then use the shortcut shown in Workbench’s Keyboard page. A narrow, mobile-looking browser page is still a Mac field.
  2. Speak and stop. The small panel keeps Stop within reach. Expand for more detail or cancellation. The panel can move without changing the destination.
  3. Check the result. Workbench reports whether text was pasted or copied. If focus changed, it copies instead. Recent transcripts keeps the original alongside the cleaned version.
Audio→Speech model→Optional cleanup→Mac field or clipboard

Two model jobs, one place to manage them.

Speech recognition turns audio into words. Workbench includes local recognition and can connect to a compatible local speech server. Text refinement works on those words afterward. Improving one does not require replacing the other.

ChoiceIntended resultRequirements
OriginalKeep the recogniser’s words, with your saved dictionary replacements. The original stays in history.No text model
LightConservative cleanup of supported fillers, spoken corrections and lists.Built-in rules; default
NaturalLocal punctuation refinement with strict word, number and list checks. If unavailable or rejected, retain conservative cleanup.Available Apple Intelligence, or an explicitly selected local Ollama model

Open Models for separate speech and text-refinement settings. Optional Ollama refinement needs Ollama running on your Mac. Choose and download a local model there, then load it when ready. Workbench does not install Ollama or silently switch to an online model. It refuses cloud-model metadata before sending text; your local server remains under your control.

Settings are captured when you start recording, import audio or begin a cleanup operation. Changing cleanup, word replacements or delivery preferences applies to the next operation. The result states which cleanup actually ran; the original remains recoverable. Model checks reduce unwanted changes but cannot guarantee perfect text.

A spelling you only need to fix once.

In Dictate, select a mistaken word or phrase and choose Remember correction…. Enter the spelling you want, check the draft preview, then save. For example, stage mark becomes StageMark in this draft and matching future dictations. With no selection, enter both phrases yourself.

The original and earlier captures stay unchanged. The receipt offers Undo; if you have since edited the draft, Undo keeps those newer edits. Saved corrections live in Dictionary. Nothing is pasted again automatically.

How close is Workbench to Flow and Superwhisper?

The basic controls are already here: local capture, small and expanded panels, cancellation, history and recovery. The larger gap is the finish around the text: contextual insertion, familiar names and first-use confidence. We explored the installed competitors’ settings and correction forms, and read their official docs; this was not a speech-accuracy benchmark.

Use Apple Shortcuts to own recording

Build Record Audio → Transcribe with Workbench → Create Note, or another text action. Apple’s action owns recording and cancellation. Workbench receives an audio file and returns text; its Mac recording panel is not a second recorder for that workflow.

FORM FACTOR MATTERS

Present the phone. Use the phone.

Connect an unlocked, trusted iPhone over USB. In Present a device, choose a scene and source, then start. The small phone tile opens by click or ⌘/. It contains presentation controls.

To enter text, tap a field on the physical phone. To dictate, tap its keyboard’s Dictation button. Tapping the field alone does not start Dictation. Workbench displays the device’s video; it does not send Mac keystrokes, clicks or microphone input back to it. Apple’s iPhone Dictation guide ↗

Mac dictation remains a separate job when you explicitly choose a Mac field. Starting it from Workbench’s phone presentation is blocked with an explanation, so it cannot appear to type on the phone.

How is this different from iPhone Mirroring or QuickTime?

Workbench can open Apple’s separate apps. iPhone Mirroring provides its own supported input flow and keeps the nearby phone locked; Apple says the phone’s microphone and camera are unavailable there. QuickTime can show a connected device. Workbench does not embed or control either app. iPhone Mirroring ↗ · QuickTime capture ↗

One card, or a prepared set.

Import finished artwork or make an editable portrait card in Personas and overlays…. Keep the simple Show one card workflow, or use Prepare presentation to place several copies and choose an ordered set of groups. Each copy can be moved, sized, hidden and locked independently. A compact click menu follows you between apps.

For a mobile presentation, place a persona inside its saved scene. These independent native overlays are for whole-display demonstrations, and are not attached to browser tabs. Download the fictional Site manager example. Use transparent PNG or WebP for a cut-out card; a baked checkerboard stays part of an imported image.

Check the audience’s view.

Whole-display sharing includes visible Workbench overlays and controls. A browser-tab share excludes separate native windows. A scene’s persona is part of the scene rendering. Teams and Zoom may capture individual windows differently; check from a receiving device before a real presentation. Workbench does not promise invisible presenter controls.

SCENE PREPARATION

A new backdrop. The same composition.

Changing a picture should keep the device, logo and persona you already positioned. In Present a device, choose Change backdrop…. Try an image from your scenes, a bundled starter or a local file. Adjust the crop, then choose Use backdrop. Cancel keeps the saved scene unchanged.

The same action repairs a missing image. It changes the saved scene; the desktop and an already-running presentation stay as they are. Add backdrop still creates a new scene.

Native checks confirmed picker cancellation, Escape, keyboard Apply, retained scene identity and missing-image repair. Archive readback confirmed unchanged foreground placement and shared originals. No live device or meeting receiver was involved.

The next step is often a missing control or a clearer handoff. These four changes use the playback, drawing, scene and library systems already in Workbench.

Read aloud: pause, move through the recording, and resume. Seeking keeps the current audio; it does not ask the voice provider to make it again. Board export: Copy board and Save board PNG include the board and its ink. To capture a browser underneath an annotation, use macOS Screenshot.

Presentation: choose Present in window beside the fullscreen action. Use the green Mac control or ⌃⌘F to switch fullscreen while the scene stays active. Select the scene window in your meeting app if useful, and check a receiver's view. Saved resources: Return copies the selected prompt or opens its link/file from search or the results list. It leaves editing fields alone and reports a failed copy honestly.

THE COMMODITY STRATEGY

Keep the jobs. Improve the engines.

One native app owns the controls and saved work. Recognition, text refinement and speech generation can improve separately. Annotation, device presentation and saved resources keep their own responsibilities. Better coding agents help maintainers deliver changes; they do not automatically change a person's app or active recording.

  1. Measure before changing a default. Compare the same inputs on the same hardware. Names, numbers, omissions, speed and failure behavior matter. Passing interface tests does not establish speech accuracy.
  2. Preserve saved work. Test older formats before changing storage. Keep malformed or unsupported future files intact and explain recovery.
  3. Extract when something needs it. Keep small boundaries. Add a shared pipeline or engine metadata when a real caller or evaluation consumes it.

What should distinguish each tool?

The advantage worth building is a dependable finished job. Individual features are easy to copy. These five directions build on the work already in the app; they are recommendations to validate, not claims of exclusive capabilities.

Keep reading retention to one deliberate item until repeated use warrants more. Validate a real library-exchange scenario before prioritising it over native Quick Look. Model engines support the voice jobs; same-input evaluations must justify changing defaults.

DESIGN RECORD

What we borrowed. What we changed.

Superwhisper’s mini and full recording views show how much room a utility can give back. Raycast’s action panel keeps actions attached to a context. Apple’s popover guidance supports small anchored panels that preserve work when dismissed.

Workbench applies those ideas with click-to-open controls, keyboard alternatives and independent positions. It does not place dictation, presentation and unrelated modes in one floating picker.

OPEN SOURCE, WITH INTENT

Small changes. A shared direction.

Ethan’s direction is shared maintainership with Matt. The working practice below is proposed for them to agree; it does not claim that GitHub permissions or branch protections enforce it. The project needs useful observations as much as code: the moment you expected something different is a good starting point.

  1. Describe the job. Open an issue with the task, expected behavior and what happened. Use made-up examples and state your Mac, macOS and app version.
  2. Agree the shape before broad work. For a new feature or surface, show the job, a sketch and what existing behavior it affects. Update the product specification with the decision.
  3. Make one reviewable change. Use a branch and pull request. Explain the resulting behavior, include screenshots for UI changes, and report the relevant checks and remaining uncertainty.
  4. Review and release deliberately. A co-maintainer reviews meaningful behavior changes. Build a Preview from the reviewed source, record its checks and checksum, then publish matching documentation.

AI assistance can write code and drafts. Maintainers remain responsible for the claims, tone, tests and decision to publish. AI-generated messages must not pretend to be a personal review by Ethan or Matt. Proposed replies stay drafts until someone authorises sending them.
